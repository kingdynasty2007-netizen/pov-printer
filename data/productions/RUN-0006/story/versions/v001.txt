She could not walk through the marketplace without hearing the words that cut deepest: the childless woman. In their village in Ephraim, every mother held a babe, every grandmother cradled grandchildren — except Hannah. Her rival, Peninnah, had sons and daughters, and she did not hide her joy at Hannah's barrenness. Day after day she provoked her, striking at the wound that never healed.

Hannah went down to the tabernacle at Shiloh and fell before the Lord. Her lips moved in silence, and tears streamed freely down her face. She poured out a vow: if God would give her a son, she would give him back — all his days, no razor would touch his head, he would belong to the Lord. The priest Eli watched her and assumed she was drunk, but when she spoke, her words revealed a grief too heavy for wine. Eli laid his blessing upon her.

That night, peace came like rain on dry ground. Her face no longer looked downcast. Months passed, and the promise took flesh within her. When Samuel was born, Hannah wept with a joy so fierce she could barely hold him. For three years she nursed him, knowing every day was borrowed time. But when the child was weaned, she did not cling to him. She gathered flour, wine, and a young bull as an offering, walked to the house of the Lord at Shiloh, and stood before Eli with the boy in her arms.

"Lord, I am the woman who stood here beside you," she said softly. "You gave me what I begged for. Now I give him back." Samuel reached for her, and for one unbearable moment she held him close. Then she set him down and let go.

Years later, when Samuel served as prophet over Israel, the people would remember not his mighty words but the woman who let him go. A good mother does not merely give life — she trusts God with it.
